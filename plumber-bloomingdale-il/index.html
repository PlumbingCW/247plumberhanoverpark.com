Sump pumps, wet basements, and Bloomingdale storms

Many Bloomingdale homes have a basement with a sump basin, and the pump is the only thing between a heavy rain and a wet floor. Pumps fail when a float sticks, the check valve clogs, or power drops during the storm. A battery backup keeps things moving when the lights go out.

If the pump runs but the water does not leave, the discharge line may be frozen or blocked. If it does not run at all, check the plug and the breaker first. Then call us for sump pump repair. Standing water in a basement needs to come out quickly to protect drywall and stored items.

What we do after the water is out

Once the basement is drained, the next step is fixing the cause. That may mean a new pump, a better discharge route, or a closer look at foundation cracks. For ongoing seepage, basement waterproofing addresses the entry points. Gutters and grading outside are often part of the cause.

Flooding can also come from the sewer side. If water rises through the floor drain instead of the sump, the problem is the sewer line, and drain cleaning or a camera check comes first.

Before We Arrive

When a basement floods, these steps help limit damage.

Cut power safely

If water is near outlets or the panel, do not wade in. Shut the breaker only from a dry spot.

Check the sump

Look at the pump, the float, and the discharge pipe outside. Note whether it runs or hums.

Lift stored items

Move boxes, rugs, and furniture to higher shelves or upstairs.

Open a path

Clear the way to the basin and the floor drain so we can start at once.

Take photos

Document the water line on walls and belongings for insurance.

Keeping the basement dry before the next storm

Test the sump pump twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the basin. The float should rise, the pump should start, and the water should empty quickly. Listen for grinding or rattling, which means a worn motor or bearing.

Outside, extend downspouts several feet from the foundation and keep window wells clear of leaves. Soil should slope away from the house. These small jobs cut the water load on the pump and reduce the chance of an overnight emergency.
