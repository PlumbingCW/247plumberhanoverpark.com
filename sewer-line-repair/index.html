Sewage in the House? Act First, Then Call

A broken sewer line can send waste into the basement or soak the yard. Stop all water use at once. That includes toilets, the washer and showers, since every gallon goes into the damaged pipe.

  1. Stop using water inside the house.
  2. Keep family and pets away from wet or flooded areas.
  3. Turn off the furnace and water heater if sewage has reached them.
  4. Call us and describe what you see, including any yard depressions.

We answer around the clock. A collapsed line cannot be cleared by cable, so we send a plumber to confirm the cause and make the house safe. After that, we walk through the repair choices.

Signs Your Sewer Pipe Is Broken

Not every backup means broken pipe. These signs together, or a camera view, tell us more.

  • Backups return soon after cleaning, in the same fixtures.
  • A wet or sunken patch of lawn over the sewer path.
  • Sewer smell in the yard or basement.
  • Cable stops at the same distance each time.
  • Cracks in the slab or floor drain gurgling after rain.
  • Rodents or insects coming from a drain.

A sewer camera settles it. It shows whether the pipe is cracked, offset, root-filled, bellied or gone. Our sewer inspection service records the footage so you can see the problem yourself.

Why Sewer Pipes Fail in Hanover Park Houses

Many homes in Greenbrook, Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem were built in the 1960s and early 1970s. The pipe that runs from those houses to the street is often clay tile or cast iron. Both materials are more than 50 years old now, and both have well-known failure patterns.

Clay tile

Clay pipe comes in short lengths with joints every few feet. Seals at those joints degrade, roots enter, and ground movement pushes sections out of line. A joint offset by an inch or two catches paper every time.

Cast iron

Cast iron corrodes from within. Scale builds up, the wall thins, and the bottom of the pipe can rot through while the top looks fine. For you, that means a line that still flows may already be lost along the invert.

Other causes

Heavy equipment over a shallow line, tree growth, freeze and thaw, and settlement can all break a pipe. Hanover Park winters add frost heave to the list. Poor original bedding also leaves bellies where water pools.

How a Sewer Repair Job Goes

Camera and locate

We record the pipe, mark depth and distance with a locator, and decide where the damage starts and ends.

Call JULIE

Illinois law requires a JULIE dig number before excavation. We request it so gas, electric and water lines are marked.

Permit and notice

Plumbing contractors must be registered with the village, and permits are not issued until they are. The village needs 24 hours notice for inspection, and work in the right of way is approved by Engineering.

Dig, cut and replace

We cut out the damaged pipe and install new six-inch pipe, bedded and sloped. We use solid wall PVC, since cellular-core PVC is not allowed.

Inspect and backfill

The inspector reviews the work before we cover it. Then we compact the soil and restore the surface as agreed.

What Hanover Park Requires for Sewer Work

The village sanitary sewer repair rules set clear minimums. The building sewer must be at least 48 inches deep, and pipe within six feet of the building must be six-inch. A cleanout comes up to grade about five feet out.

Work within five feet of the building must be done by a licensed plumber. The rules also bar cellular-core PVC. In practice, a repair that ignores them can fail inspection and get dug up a second time.

Lining versus digging

Lining can avoid a trench. The village allows it only when the pipe is descaled first and a recorded camera inspection supports it, and it is not allowed on defective pipe. If the camera shows a collapsed or badly offset section, digging is likely.

Flood-prone areas

Hazard planning for the village names the Highlands subdivision, Schick Road, Anne Fox School Creek, and the Barrington and Irving Park Road area as flood-prone. In these areas, a backup after heavy rain can come from stormwater entering the sewer. Fixing joints helps, and a backwater valve is worth discussing with us.

Verify any plumber you hire

Illinois licenses plumbers through IDPH. The 058 license is the plumber license, and 055 is the contractor registration. IDPH has an online lookup, and the village requires registration before it issues permits.

What Affects the Cost of Sewer Line Repair

A sewer repair is priced after a camera inspection, since depth and length decide most of the work. These are the factors that matter most.

Depth and length of the damage

A short section near the cleanout is a small job. A long run at the village minimum depth or deeper takes more digging.

Repair method

Spot repair, trench replacement, bursting and lining each use different equipment, crew time and materials.

Surface above the pipe

Lawn is cheaper to restore than a driveway, a patio or a tree-lined bed.

Permits and inspections

Village permits, JULIE locates, and inspection scheduling all take time and are part of the job.

Pipe condition elsewhere

If the rest of the line is poor, replacing more now avoids a second dig later.

Insurance often does not cover wear and tear or root damage. Ask your insurer whether any sewer backup endorsement applies, and keep the camera footage.

Cleaning, Repair or Replacement

Some lines need only cleaning. If the camera shows a sound pipe with a grease or paper blockage, our sewer line cleaning work may be all you need. If roots are the whole problem and the pipe is intact, hydro jetting followed by a recheck can delay a dig.

A clay pipe with several offset joints, or a cast iron pipe with a rotted bottom, usually needs replacement. Repairing one joint while the next three fail wastes a dig. We use the footage to show where the damage ends and explain the sensible scope.

If the damaged pipe connects to a basement that floods, review your sump pump setup as well. A sump discharge should never be tied into a failing sanitary line.

Planning for Landscaping and Access

Mark your sprinkler heads, invisible fence wire and any buried lines you know about. Tell us where trees stand, since roots affect the route. Move vehicles from the driveway if it will be cut.

Excavation is messy but short. After backfill, the soil settles, so a patch of lawn may sink slightly for a season. We discuss restoration before we start so nothing surprises you.

Local note

Records You Should Ask For

Keep the permit number, the camera footage and the inspection record from any sewer repair. When you sell the house, a buyer's inspector may scan the line, and clear records show what was done.

The village keeps permit files at Inspectional Services, open Monday through Friday from 8 to 4:30 and closed on Wednesdays. For you, asking them for history on your address can show earlier sewer work before you plan a new repair.
