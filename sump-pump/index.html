Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Hanover Park, IL

When a sump pump fails during a storm, the basement can take on water fast. Call at the first sign.

What to do right now if the pit is filling

Look at the sump basin. If water is near the rim and the pump is silent, check that it is plugged in and that the breaker has not tripped. Do not stand in water while checking electrical items.

If the basement floor is already wet, lift valuables off the floor and stay out of any area where water touches outlets or the panel. Call us right away. A bucket brigade will not keep pace with groundwater, but moving boxes and furniture will save property.

Power outage during a storm?

A standard pump stops when the power goes out. A battery backup keeps it running for a limited time, so check it before storm season.

Why sump pumps fail

Stuck float switch

The float can jam against the basin wall or hang on its tether. The pump either never starts or never shuts off.

Burned-out motor

Pumps that run constantly overheat. Most last about ten years, and many fail when they are needed most.

Failed check valve

The one-way valve on the discharge line stops water running back into the pit. If it fails, the pump cycles again and again.

Clogged intake or impeller

Silt, gravel and debris jam the impeller. A pump that hums without moving water is the usual sign.

Frozen or blocked discharge

Ice at the outlet in winter, or a crushed line outside, keeps water from leaving. The motor runs hot and trips.

Wrong size or old basin

A pump too small for the inflow runs endlessly. A basin that is cracked or too shallow short-cycles the unit.

How we repair or replace a sump pump

Pull and test the pump

We lift the pump from the basin, check the motor, the impeller and the float, and test it in a bucket of water.

Inspect the basin and check valve

We look for silt, a cracked basin and a check valve that leaks back. These are cheap parts, and they cause expensive failures.

Check the discharge line

We follow the pipe outside to confirm it runs away from the foundation and is not frozen or crushed.

Add or test backup power

A battery backup or water-powered unit covers a power failure. We test it under load and set the float heights.

What affects sump pump repair cost

A float fix and a new pump with backup are very different jobs. These factors decide the scope.

Repair or replace

A new float or check valve is a small job. A burned motor or a cracked basin points to replacement.

Pump type and horsepower

A bigger submersible or a cast iron unit costs more than a basic thermoplastic one.

Basin and pit condition

Silt, a cracked liner or a pit that is too small affects how the pump runs.

Discharge route

Longer or buried lines, extra elbows and frost risk add work.

Backup system

Battery backup, alarm and water-powered options each add parts and setup time.

Keeping the pump ready for storm season

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch it run. The pump should start, empty the basin and shut off cleanly. Do this each spring, and again after heavy rain.

If you also see damp walls, cracks or musty smells, the pump is only part of the solution. Sealing the walls and managing groundwater around the foundation may call for basement waterproofing. For a sewer backup that rises through the floor drain, call us as well, and see sewer line repair. A basement bathroom that backs up during storms may also need a toilet repair check.
