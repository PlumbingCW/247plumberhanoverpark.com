Gas Water Heater Replacement Cost in Hanover Park, IL

No two gas water heater swaps cost the same. These are the factors that move the total, so you can compare quotes sensibly.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

What actually sets the price of a gas water heater swap

Five things decide most of the bill: the tank size and efficiency, the venting that already exists, the gas piping, the code upgrades your home needs, and the permit. The heater itself is only part of the total. Labor and fittings often change the number more than the tank does.

We do not post dollar figures here because they would be wrong for your house by next month. A like-for-like swap in an easy spot will cost far less than a move to a new location with new vent work. Ask any plumber to break the quote into those same five pieces.

Tank size and efficiency

A larger tank costs more to buy and weighs more to carry. Common household sizes run 40 to 50 gallons, and families with several bathrooms may need more. Check the nameplate on your current unit for capacity and gas input, because the village requires both to be on the new label.

Efficiency rating matters too. A standard atmospheric heater uses a draft hood and a metal flue. A higher-efficiency power-vent or condensing model uses a fan and plastic pipe, and costs more up front. Lower fuel use can pay that back over the life of the unit.

Venting is the biggest wild card

Gas heaters must exhaust combustion gases outdoors, and the vent type has to match the heater. Swapping an atmospheric model for a power-vent one means new piping through a wall or the roof. That adds materials, labor, and sometimes a masonry or framing repair.

Old flues also need inspection. A rusted or undersized chimney liner can pull exhaust back into the house. Carbon monoxide is the reason the village asks for alarms outside each sleeping area when a fuel-burning appliance is replaced.

Gas line, shutoff, and combustion air

A sound gas line and a shutoff valve within reach are basic needs. Corroded flex connectors are replaced, not reused. Some basements and closets lack enough combustion air, so the installer may add a louver or duct to feed the burner.

Hanover Park's water heater requirements also call for a #8 grounding jumper across the hot water, cold water, and gas piping. It is a small part, but it must be there on the inspection day.

Code upgrades that add parts

Most older installs miss at least one current requirement. Common additions include a new temperature and pressure relief valve drain line, a drain pan, a vacuum relief valve, and an expansion tank. Each one is a small part, but they add up.

The relief valve drain must run to within six inches of the floor in galvanized steel or copper pipe. It cannot be reduced in size, cannot be threaded at the end, and cannot extend outdoors. A plastic line that was fine years ago will fail inspection today.

Permit and inspection

Hanover Park requires a permit for a water heater replacement. A licensed plumber submits the application with a letter of intent and the manufacturer specification sheet. Permit fees and any correction trips are part of the cost, so ask whether they are included.

After the work, the village schedules an inspection with at least 24 hours of notice. An adult must be home, because the inspector checks the service panel as well as the heater.

Reading a quote

A fair quote lists the heater model and size, the vent work, the gas fittings, the relief valve and drain, the expansion tank if needed, the permit, and haul-away. Vague quotes with one lump number hide the pieces that vary. If a price looks far below others, ask what was left out.

Compare the same scope, not just the total. A cheap bid that skips the permit or reuses an old flex connector may cost more later when the inspector flags it.

Repair or replace

A leaking tank cannot be fixed. A failed thermostat, igniter, or thermocouple often can. If the unit is past its useful life or rusting at the bottom, replacement makes more sense than another repair. See water heater repair first if the tank is still dry.

When replacement is the answer, the water heater replacement team can size the new unit to your household. Your Hanover Park home's hot water use is the starting point, not the size of the old tank.

Ways to keep the cost down without cutting corners

Pick the right size, not the biggest one. Oversized tanks cost more to buy and more to keep hot. Ask for a quote that keeps the heater near the existing vent and gas line, since moving it is the quickest way to raise the total.

Have the area cleared before the crew arrives. A heater tucked behind storage shelves or down a narrow stair slows the job, and slow jobs cost more. Flush the new tank each year and check the anode rod to stretch its life.

Finally, ask whether a power-vent or condensing model fits your budget over ten years, not just on day one. Fuel savings can offset a higher purchase price in a busy household, but only if the venting works out.

What the village inspection means for your bill

The Village of Hanover Park publishes a one-page water heater replacement checklist. It requires a permit, a licensed plumber's signed agreement and letter of intent, the manufacturer's specification sheet, a relief valve drain within six inches of the floor, and an expansion tank when the water supply is protected by an RPZ valve.

Plan for those items in the quote. A plumber who knows the checklist will price the drain line, grounding jumper, and pan up front, and the inspection call goes smoothly. A quote that ignores them may bring surprise add-ons after the inspector visits.
