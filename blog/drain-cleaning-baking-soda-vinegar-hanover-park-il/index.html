Does baking soda and vinegar clear a drain?

It can freshen a drain and loosen a thin film of grease. It will not clear a real clog, especially in old cast iron or galvanized pipe.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Call (630) 822-9290

The short answer

Baking soda and vinegar can loosen a light film of soap and grease near the drain opening. They cannot dissolve hair, wipes, or a wad of food waste. If water is standing in the sink or tub, the clog is deeper than the fizz can reach.

In Hanover Park, many homes date from the 1960s and 1970s. Their drain lines are rougher inside than new pipe, so debris catches more easily. That is why a home remedy that works in a newer house often fails here.

What the fizz actually does

Baking soda is a mild base and vinegar is a mild acid. Together they make carbon dioxide gas, water, and a little sodium acetate. The bubbling looks powerful, but the mixture is mostly water by the time it settles in the trap.

The gas can nudge loose a thin coating on the pipe wall. It does not push a blockage down the line, and it does not break up hair. Think of it as a rinse, not a repair.

How to try it without making things worse

  1. Bail out any standing water so the mixture can reach the clog.
  2. Pour half a cup of baking soda into the drain, then half a cup of white vinegar.
  3. Plug the opening and wait about fifteen minutes while it fizzes.
  4. Flush with hot tap water. Use warm water only if your trap or pipe is PVC.
  5. Run the tap for a minute and watch whether the water drains at a normal speed.

Never mix vinegar with a bleach cleaner or an ammonia cleaner. The combination can release harmful gas. If you already poured a store-bought opener, skip the vinegar and wait for the pipe to clear on its own.

Why old cast iron and galvanized pipe fight back

Cast iron develops rough rust nodules over decades. Those bumps catch hair and grease, and the opening narrows a little each year. A cup of vinegar cannot remove rust scale that has hardened into the pipe wall.

Galvanized steel supply and drain lines corrode from the inside. The zinc coating wears away, rust builds up, and the passage shrinks. Homeowners often see a drain that clogs again within days of every home treatment.

When the same drain keeps slowing down, the pipe itself is the problem. A professional drain cleaning with a cable scrapes the wall, and hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour off grease and scale. A camera can show whether the pipe is sound enough for either method.

Signs it is time to call a plumber

  • Water backs up in a second fixture, such as a tub gurgling when the washer drains.
  • The drain clears for a day, then slows again.
  • You smell sewage near a floor drain or basement fixture.
  • A toilet bubbles or rises when you run the sink.
  • More than one drain in the house is slow at the same time.

Several slow drains at once point to the main line, not a single trap. That is a job for a cable machine or a jetter, and sometimes a camera. Our crews also work in neighboring Streamwood and Schaumburg, where many homes share the same era of pipe.

Safer habits that keep drains open

Use a hair catcher in every tub and shower. Scrape plates into the trash, not the sink. Pour cooking grease into a can and throw it away once it hardens.

Flush only toilet paper. Even wipes labeled flushable hold together long enough to snag on rough cast iron. A monthly hot water rinse is fine, but it is maintenance, not a cure.

What a plumber does that a home mix cannot

A drain cleaning machine feeds a rotating steel cable into the pipe until it meets the blockage. The cutting head breaks through or hooks the clog and scrapes the pipe wall on the way out. This works on hair, roots, and compacted paper.

For heavy grease or scale, a jetter sends water through a nozzle at high pressure. The spray scours the whole circumference of the pipe instead of punching a single hole. A camera run afterward shows whether the pipe is cracked, bellied, or offset.

That inspection is the real value. Cast iron that is flaking or clay that has shifted will clog again no matter how clean it looks today. Knowing the condition lets you decide between regular cleaning and a repair.

Kitchen, bathroom, and basement drains behave differently

Kitchen drains clog with grease, coffee grounds, and food. Bathroom drains clog with hair and soap. A basement floor drain clogs with lint, silt, and whatever washed down the laundry line.

The fizz is most likely to help in the bathroom sink, where the clog is a soft film near the opening. It is least likely to help on a basement floor drain, where the blockage sits far down a larger line.

Safety before you start

Baking soda and vinegar are mild, but never follow them with a commercial drain cleaner, because mixed chemicals can release harmful fumes. Do not pour boiling water into a cracked or loose porcelain fixture, and keep the room ventilated. If any chemical gets on skin or eyes, rinse with water and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222.

Codes and standards worth knowing

Drain repairs in Illinois follow the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). Check any plumber you hire against the Illinois Department of Public Health license lookup.
