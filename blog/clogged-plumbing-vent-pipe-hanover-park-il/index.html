How to tell if your plumbing vent pipe is clogged

Gurgling drains, bubbling toilets, and a faint sewer smell often trace back to a blocked vent on the roof. Winter frost can close one overnight.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Call (630) 822-9290

What the vent pipe does

Every drain in your house connects to a vent that runs up through the roof. The vent lets air in behind the water as it flows out, so the drain empties smoothly. It also lets sewer gas escape above the roofline instead of into your rooms.

When the vent is blocked, the drain has to pull air from somewhere else. It pulls it through the trap, and that is when you hear gurgling.

Signs the vent is clogged

  • Gurgling or glugging sounds when a tub, sink, or washer drains.
  • Slow drains at several fixtures with no clog in the trap.
  • A toilet that bubbles or flushes weakly.
  • A sewer smell inside the house, since the trap seal gets pulled dry.
  • Water that drains in surges, then pauses.

One slow drain is usually a local clog. Several symptoms at once, especially gurgling in more than one fixture, point to the vent or the main drain. A plumber can tell which one with a cable and a camera.

What blocks a vent on the roof

Leaves and seeds fall into the open pipe, especially from trees overhanging the roof. Birds and squirrels build nests inside the opening, and wasps sometimes do the same. A tennis ball or other debris can drop in as well.

Collapsed or crushed vent pipe in the attic is another cause. So is a vent that was tied into the wrong place during an old remodel. These problems need a plumber, not just a garden hose.

Why winter can close a vent

In a cold spell, warm moist air rising from the house meets freezing air at the top of the vent. The moisture condenses and freezes into a ring of frost that narrows the opening. In a hard freeze, the ring can grow until the pipe is nearly shut.

The effect is worse on small-diameter vents. If your gurgling shows up only on the coldest mornings and fades by afternoon, frost closure is a strong suspect. Do not climb an icy roof to check it.

What you can check safely

  1. Run water in each fixture and note which ones gurgle.
  2. Look at the vent from the ground with binoculars, if you can see it.
  3. Pour a bucket of water into a floor drain to see whether the trap refills quietly.
  4. Check whether the problem changes with temperature or wind.
  5. Call a plumber if the noise continues or the smell does not go away.

Running a hose down a vent can clear loose leaves, but only if you can reach the roof safely. A hose will not clear a nest packed tight, and it will not fix a collapsed pipe.

When a vent problem is really a sewer problem

A blocked main line causes similar symptoms, including gurgling and slow drains. If the lowest fixture in the house backs up first, the problem is in the drain. In that case drain cleaning or sewer inspection with a camera is the right next step.

How a plumber finds and clears the blockage

The plumber starts by listening to the drains and checking each fixture. If the vent is the suspect, the roof termination is inspected for nests, leaves, or ice. A small cable or a water line is fed down the vent to break up the blockage.

In some homes the vent can be reached from the attic or through a cleanout. A camera may be used to see inside the pipe and look for a crushed section. The plumber then tests every drain again to confirm that the gurgling is gone.

Preventing future vent blockages

Trim branches that overhang the roof near the vent. Install a vent cap only if the code allows it, because the wrong cap can trap frost. Have the roof termination looked at when you replace shingles or flashing.

If frost closure keeps returning, ask the plumber about enlarging the pipe diameter above the roof. The wider opening takes longer to freeze shut. This is a small change that often solves a winter-only problem.

Other causes inside the house

Not every vent problem is on the roof. A vent inside the wall can be pinched during a remodel or filled with insulation. A vent that was sloped the wrong way can collect condensation and hold water.

Air admittance valves under sinks are another source. They open to let air in and close to hold gas out. When one sticks, the sink gurgles and the cabinet smells, and the valve is easy to replace.

A plumber can test each one by listening and by smoke or pressure testing. Fixing the right piece saves you from cutting open walls that did not need it.

After the repair, run every fixture at once for a minute and listen. Quiet drains and a steady flow tell you the vent is open again. Keep a note of the date, so you can tell a plumber if the noise returns.

A quiet drain after a cold snap is a good sign. Check again after the next hard freeze to be sure the opening stays clear.

Safety before you start

Do not climb onto a roof to clear a vent in winter, in the rain or alone. If you smell sewer gas indoors, open windows, avoid flames and call us. If anyone feels dizzy or ill, leave the home and call 911.
