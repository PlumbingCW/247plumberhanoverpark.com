Main Sewer Line Inspection in Hanover Park: What the Camera Shows

A camera inspection runs a waterproof lens through your sewer to show roots, cracks, bellies and breaks, so repairs are based on what is really there.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Call (630) 822-9290

What a sewer camera inspection is

A main sewer line inspection feeds a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable into your building sewer, usually through a cleanout. You watch the live picture on a monitor while the operator records it. The goal is to see the pipe from the inside, find defects and mark their location.

It is worth doing when you have slow drains in several fixtures, repeated backups, gurgling toilets or you are buying a house. For problems that point to the main line, see our sewer inspection service.

What the camera can find

Defects a camera can show and what they mean
What you seeWhat it usually meansLikely next step
Roots entering at jointsPipe joints are open and seeking waterCut roots, then consider lining or repair
Belly or low spotPipe has settled and holds standing waterDig and re-grade the section
Offset jointTwo pipe sections have shifted apartSpot repair or replacement
Cracks or breaksPipe has failed from age, soil movement or loadRepair or replace the damaged run
Scale or grease build-upPipe is narrowed on the insideDescale or jet the line
Collapsed sectionClay or cast iron has fallen inExcavation

Clay tile and cast iron pipe, common in homes built before the 1970s, tend to show root intrusion and flaking inside. Orangeburg fiber pipe, used in some mid-century houses, can look collapsed or oval. The recording lets you see all of this, not just hear about it.

How the inspection works, step by step

Locate the cleanout

The operator finds the cleanout, which may be inside near the foundation or outside in the yard. If there is none, the camera may go in through a toilet flange or roof vent.

Feed the camera

The cable is pushed through slowly. A built-in transmitter lets the operator track depth and position from above.

Record the run

The video is saved with distance markers. Notes show where any problem sits relative to the house.

Review together

You see the footage and learn what is cosmetic and what needs work. A clean pipe with only minor wear may need nothing.

Some inspectors cannot pass a heavy blockage. In that case, the line must be cleared first. Our sewer line cleaning work often comes before the camera when the pipe is packed with roots or grease.

What an inspection does not tell you

A camera shows the pipe interior but cannot say how the soil outside is behaving. It cannot see a leak that is just a hairline unless water is moving through it. It cannot measure the pipe's strength. These limits mean a serious finding is often followed by a second look or a dye test.

Also remember the camera only covers the run it reaches. If the line connects to a shared lateral or a municipal main past the property, the village handles the sections in its right of way.

How to prepare for the inspection

Clear access to the cleanout, indoors or out. Move stored boxes away from basement walls near the foundation. Keep pets inside while the crew works. If the cleanout cap is stuck, tell the operator in advance so the right tools come along.

Avoid running laundry or long showers right before the visit. High flow in the line makes it harder for the camera to see the pipe floor. Let the operator know about any past backups, recent cleanings, or work done on the line, since each detail helps interpret the video.

After the inspection, ask for a short written summary with distances to each problem. Distances from the cleanout let a repair crew dig in the right place and save you from open trenches you do not need.

Signs your main line needs a look

  • Slow drains in more than one fixture at once
  • Gurgling toilets after the washer drains
  • Sewage smell in the basement or yard
  • Wet, sunken or unusually green patches over the sewer path
  • Floor drain backing up during heavy rain

One slow sink is usually a local clog. Several fixtures acting up together point to the main line, and that is the moment a camera pays for itself.

Local note

Village rules that shape the next step

Hanover Park has specific rules for sanitary sewer repairs. Work within 5 feet of a building needs a licensed plumber, and a JULIE dig number is required before digging. The building sewer must be at least 48 inches deep, and a 6 inch pipe is required within 6 feet of the building. A cleanout must be brought to grade about 5 feet out, and cellular-core PVC is not allowed.

Relining a sewer requires descaling and a recorded camera inspection first, and it is not allowed on defective pipe. The village asks for 24 hours notice for an inspection, and right-of-way work is approved and inspected by village Engineering. The practical meaning is that your video is the paper trail. Keep the footage, because it may decide whether you can reline or must dig.

Source type: Village of Hanover Park sanitary sewer repair requirements

What to do with the results

If the camera finds a minor issue such as light scale or a few fine roots, routine cleaning may be enough. If it finds a belly, offset or break, you will probably need sewer line repair by excavation or a trenchless method where it is permitted. Get the work scoped from the recording, and ask about permits before the crew starts.

Homeowners in Bartlett and Schaumburg with 1960s and 1970s houses deal with the same pipe types, so the same camera steps apply. In Hanover Park, much of the housing dates to the 1960s and 1970s, so clay and cast iron building sewers are common enough to inspect before they fail.

How often should you inspect

If your house is more than 40 years old and has mature trees near the sewer, a camera check every few years is sensible. After any backup, inspect before you pay for a repeat cleaning. When you buy a home, schedule it during due diligence so you see problems before closing.

Codes and standards worth knowing

Village rules for sanitary sewer repair, which any inspection finding feeds into, include a JULIE dig number (800-892-0123), a 48 inch minimum depth and a recorded camera inspection before relining.
