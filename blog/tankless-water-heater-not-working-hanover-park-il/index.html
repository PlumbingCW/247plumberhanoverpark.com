Tankless Water Heater Not Working in Hanover Park, IL

Cold water, an error code, or a unit that shuts off mid-shower usually has a short list of causes. Here is how to sort them.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Call (630) 822-9290

Start with the display

Most tankless heaters show a numeric code when something is wrong. Write the code down before you reset anything, and check the manual for your brand. The code tells you which system failed: air, gas, flame, water flow, or temperature.

Common causes of no hot water are a blocked vent, a closed gas valve, scale inside the heat exchanger, a flow too low to fire the burner, or a full condensate trap. The sections below take them in order, easiest first.

Quick checks before any code

  • Confirm the unit has power and the display is lit.
  • Check that the gas shutoff is open and other gas appliances work.
  • Make sure the cold water valve and hot water isolation valves are open.
  • Look for a tripped breaker or a GFCI outlet that needs reset.
  • Open a hot tap fully to see if the unit fires.
  • Check that nothing blocks the vent terminal outside.

A tankless unit only runs when it senses water moving. If it does not start at one faucet but does at another, the problem is flow or a clogged fixture, not the heater.

What common error codes mean

Codes vary by brand, so use your own manual. The examples below come from Rinnai's technical bulletin on condensing models and show the pattern most makers follow.

Sample Rinnai condensing heater codes
CodeMeaningFirst thing to check
10Air supply or exhaust blockageVent terminal, vent joints, and fan
11No ignitionGas supply, igniter, and burner
12Flame failureGas pressure, flue outlet, and flame rod
16Over temperature warningAirflow, low flow, and heat exchanger
25Condensate trap errorCondensate drain and trap
52Modulating valve signal abnormalValve wiring harness

Codes 10 and 25 are the ones owners can often clear themselves, by freeing a blocked vent or condensate drain. Codes 11, 12, and 52 point to gas or electrical parts that need a technician.

Scale buildup and the flush

Hard water leaves mineral scale inside the heat exchanger. The coating cuts heat transfer, raises exhaust temperature, and can trigger overheat codes or a weak, lukewarm output. A unit that was fine last year may start to cycle on and off.

Flushing removes it. Close the isolation valves, connect hoses to the service ports, and circulate white vinegar or a descaling solution through the unit with a small pump for about 45 minutes. Then rinse with clean water and open the valves.

Most makers advise a flush about once a year in hard water areas. Skipping it is the most common reason tankless units fail early. Clean the inlet water filter screen at the same time.

Flow rate: why the burner will not fire

Every tankless model has a minimum flow, often around half a gallon per minute, below which the burner will not light. Low-flow fixtures, a clogged aerator, or a partly closed valve can sit under that threshold.

The reverse also happens. If you run two showers and a washer at once, demand can exceed what the unit can heat, and the water turns lukewarm. Check the rated flow on your model against what the house is asking for.

A cold water sandwich, a short burst of cold between hot slugs, is a normal trait of tankless systems and not a fault. Short taps and long pipe runs make it worse.

Gas supply problems

Tankless heaters pull a large volume of gas when they fire. An undersized line, a partly closed valve, or low pressure can starve the burner and cause ignition or flame codes. This often shows up after a second gas appliance is added.

Do not adjust gas pressure or open the burner area yourself. If you smell gas, leave the house, then call the gas utility and emergency services from outside. Gas work belongs to a licensed plumber.

Condensate drain and vent issues

Condensing tankless models make acidic water that drains through a small trap and a plastic line. Debris or a kinked hose can back it up, trip a condensate code, and shut the unit down. Clear the trap and make sure the line slopes toward the drain.

In winter, an outdoor vent terminal can be blocked by snow, and a poorly routed condensate line can freeze. Keep the terminal clear and the line inside conditioned space.

Power, ignition, and sensors

A tankless heater needs steady power for its fan, controller, and igniter. A loose cord, a tripped GFCI outlet, or a power surge can shut it down. Check the outlet with a lamp or phone charger before blaming the heater.

Flame rods and thermistors are small sensors that wear with age. A dirty flame rod causes flame failure codes, and a bad temperature sensor causes swings between hot and cold. Cleaning or replacing them is quick for a technician but not something to guess at.

If the display is blank, check the breaker first. A dead board is rare, but a dead outlet is common, and it costs nothing to rule out.

When to call a plumber

Call for tankless water heater service if codes return after a reset, the unit smells of gas, or there is water at the base. Ignition and flame faults need parts and gas testing, which is licensed work.

If the unit is old and failing repeatedly, ask about water heater replacement. Neighbors in Bloomingdale and Schaumburg have the same concerns, since scale and gas supply are the usual culprits.

Codes and standards worth knowing

Tankless units are installed and serviced to the maker's published guidelines together with the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). Gas and venting work should not be improvised.
