Shower faucet repair: handles, diverters, and low flow

Not every shower problem is a dripping cartridge. Loose handles, stuck diverters, weak spray, and mineral scale each have their own cause and part.

By the Hanover Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Call (630) 822-9290

Match the problem to the part

Most shower complaints come from a handful of small parts. Each part fails in a predictable way. If you know the symptom, you can usually name the part before you pick up a tool.

Loose handles and cracked trim

A handle that spins without moving the water is usually stripped. The tiny set screw may be loose, or the splines in the handle may have worn smooth. Tighten the screw first, then replace the handle or adapter if it slips again.

The trim plate, or escutcheon, covers the hole in the wall. If it is cracked or the gasket behind it is missing, water can run into the wall cavity. Replace the trim and run a bead of silicone along the top and sides, leaving the bottom edge open so any water can drain out.

Diverters that will not hold

A diverter sends water to the tub spout or up to the shower head. On a tub with a lift knob on the spout, the rubber gasket inside wears out, and water sprays from the spout while the shower runs. Replacing the spout is a quick job on most tubs.

On a three-handle valve, the middle handle is the diverter. It can wear like any other stem, and the repair is a new stem or washer. A cartridge diverter may need to be pulled and matched at a supplier.

Low flow and hard water scale

Hard water leaves white or tan scale on shower heads, stems, and cartridges. The scale clogs nozzles first, then slows the cartridge. Soaking a removed shower head in white vinegar for a few hours often brings the spray back.

New shower heads include a flow restrictor, which is a small disc that limits water use. Federal rules cap shower heads at 2.5 gallons per minute. Removing a restrictor may increase flow, but you should check local rules and manufacturer guidance first.

If flow is weak at every fixture in the house, the cause is not the shower. Look at the main shutoff, the pressure reducing valve, or a corroded supply line. A plumber can test the pressure with a gauge.

When scale means a bigger fix

Heavy scale inside the valve can score the cartridge seat. A cartridge replacement then fails quickly because the surface is rough. In that case the valve body may need to be replaced, and a plumber should do that behind the wall.

A whole-house water softener reduces scale on every fixture. If you are replacing a valve anyway, faucet repair with a new trim kit gives you a chance to fix other worn parts. We also serve Schaumburg and Bloomingdale with the same repairs.

Buying the right replacement parts

Shower parts are not universal. Cartridges, trim kits, and diverter stems are made to fit a specific brand and model. Copying the model number from the valve body is the surest way to buy the right part.

If the valve is old and the brand is worn off, bring the old part to a plumbing supplier. Many parts can be matched by the stem length, the number of splines, and the diameter. Avoid cheap universal kits that rely on adapters.

When a full valve replacement is the better choice

Replacing a valve means opening the wall, soldering or crimping new connections, and sealing the tile. It costs more time than a cartridge swap, but it removes the root problem. A plumber may suggest it when the valve is pitted, the stops fail, or parts are no longer made.

In an older home, the supply lines behind the wall are worth a look at the same time. If they show heavy corrosion, repiping that section may save a second wall opening later.

Cleaning scale off a shower head

Fill a plastic bag with white vinegar, slip it over the shower head, and secure it with a rubber band. Let it soak for a few hours, then wipe the face with a soft brush. Rinse by running the water for a minute.

Use a toothpick to clear any nozzle that is still blocked. Never use a wire or a metal pick on rubber nozzles, because it can tear them. If the head is a thin plastic type, a replacement costs less effort than repeated cleaning.

Do the same to the aerators on bathroom faucets. They collect the same scale, and weak flow there is often mistaken for a pressure problem.

Keep a small kit on hand with a spare set screw, plumber's grease, and a roll of thread seal tape. Most handle and trim repairs need only these items, and having them avoids a second trip to the store while the shower is out of service. Write down the valve brand and model on the inside of a cabinet door for next time.

Scale and wear build slowly, so a quick look each season catches trouble early.

Local note

Hardness and the Hanover Park water supply

Hanover Park gets 100 percent Lake Michigan water through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency, and Chicago treats it. The village reports show no hardness figure. Streamwood's 2023 report, for the same Chicago supply, lists hardness as 8 grains per gallon, or 128 to 144 mg/L as calcium carbonate.

Water at that level is moderately hard, so expect white scale on shower heads and aerators over time. A vinegar soak every few months keeps the spray even, and hardness figures from a test strip can help you decide about a softener.

Source type: Streamwood 2023 water quality report (same Chicago supply) and Village of Hanover Park

Codes and standards worth knowing

Shower valve work follows the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890), which also governs anti-scald and fixture requirements. Confirm a plumber's license with the Illinois Department of Public Health.
