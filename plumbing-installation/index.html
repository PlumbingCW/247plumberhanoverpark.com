What plumbing installation covers

Installation is plumbing that adds something new or replaces an existing fixture or appliance. It is planned work, unlike an emergency repair, so there is time to choose parts and sort out permits. The most common jobs we handle are listed below.

  • Kitchen sinks, faucets and garbage disposals
  • Toilets, vanities, tubs and showers
  • Washing machine and dishwasher hookups
  • Gas lines and supply for ranges, dryers and grills
  • Outdoor spigots and frost-free hose bibs
  • Basement bathrooms, wet bars and laundry rooms
  • Water heaters, covered under water heater installation

Each job has its own supply, drain and vent needs. A fixture that drains slowly or gurgles often traces back to a venting or slope mistake made during installation, so getting these details right at the start matters.

How we plan an installation

Review the site

We look at existing supply, drain and vent lines, shutoff valves and the distance to the nearest stack.

Check the code

We confirm what the Illinois Plumbing Code requires for fixture spacing, venting, trap size and backflow protection.

Pull the permit

If the work needs one, we file through Inspectional Services and schedule inspections.

Install and connect

We rough in lines, set fixtures, make supply and drain connections and test for leaks.

Inspect and finish

The village inspects where required, and we go over the fixtures and shutoffs with you.

Vents, traps and backflow protection

A drain without a vent will gurgle and may siphon the trap dry, letting sewer gas into the room. The Illinois Plumbing Code sets trap sizes, vent distances and slope for drains. We follow those rules so a fixture drains cleanly the day it is installed.

Backflow protection keeps dirty water from being pulled back into the clean supply. The code calls for vacuum breakers, anti-siphon fill valves and air gaps in specific cases, such as toilet tanks, lawn sprinklers and hose outlets. Outdoor spigots need protection, and hose bibs that can freeze need to be frost-protected. If a fixture or sprinkler line has no backflow device, we add one.

Basements and below-grade fixtures

Adding a basement bathroom means getting waste up to the sewer. If the building drain is above the fixture, a sewage ejector pump lifts it. If the drain is below, gravity may be enough. Either way, the basin needs a sealed lid and a vent, and the pump needs a check valve.

Hanover Park has a flood history, and the Cook County hazard mitigation plan lists flood-prone areas such as the Highlands subdivision, Schick Road and the Barrington and Irving Park Road corridors. If you plan below-grade plumbing in those areas, ask us about a backwater valve and a battery backup for the pump. A sump pump or basement waterproofing may also belong in the plan.

Working with the homes in Hanover Park

Hanover Park's housing is largely 1960s and 1970s, with older galvanized or copper supply and cast iron or clay building sewers. Tying new fixtures into old pipe takes care. Connecting copper to galvanized without a dielectric fitting speeds corrosion, and adding fixtures to an old drain can expose a weak or undersized line.

The village's Lake Michigan water has moderate hardness, according to reports for the same Chicago supply. Scale builds up on new faucets and showerheads, so some customers add a water softener during a remodel when the pipes are already open. If you are adding a drain line, a drain cleaning check first can reveal problems in the main.

What affects the cost of a plumbing installation

The price of an install depends on what is behind the wall as much as on the fixture. These are the main factors.

Distance to existing lines

A fixture next to the old one is simple. Moving to a new wall or floor needs new rough-in.

Fixture and fittings you choose

A basic faucet and a custom shower valve with body sprays are very different jobs.

Access

Open framing is easy. Finished walls, slabs and tight chases take more work.

Condition of existing pipe

Corroded valves, undersized drains or brittle supply lines may need replacement.

Permits and inspections

New lines and relocations typically need a village permit and an inspection.

Planned installations and upgrades are generally paid out of pocket and are not an insured loss. Insurance only enters if a failure causes sudden water damage.

Local note

Permits and licensed plumbers in Hanover Park

Plumbing permits are issued by the Inspectional Services Division, 2121 Lake Street, Monday to Friday from 8 to 4:30, closed Wednesdays. Contractors must be registered with the village, with IDPH 055 contractor registration and a 058 plumber licence, and permits are not issued until registration is complete.

Owner-occupants can sign a Homeowner Plumbing Affidavit and do their own work, but may only hire Illinois-licensed plumbers to help. If you want an inspection to pass the first time, hiring a registered plumber for the rough-in is the safer path.

Source type: Hanover Park Inspectional Services and Illinois IDPH

Codes and standards that apply

New fixtures and piping are regulated by the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). Illinois also lets owner-occupants do their own work with a Homeowner Plumbing Affidavit, but they may hire only Illinois-licensed plumbers to assist.
