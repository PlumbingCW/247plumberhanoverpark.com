What changes from town to town

  • Water supply: Hanover Park, Streamwood and Schaumburg take Chicago water through the joint action agency, while Bartlett, Bloomingdale and Roselle are listed as DuPage Water Commission partners.
  • Housing age: many Hanover Park and Streamwood homes date from the 1960s and 1970s, so older pipe is common. Neighborhoods built later have newer materials and different failure points.
  • Permits: each village has its own inspection office and rules, and plumbers must register before pulling a permit.
  • Flood risk: low-lying streets near creeks need sump pumps and clear floor drains, which is why basement calls rise after heavy storms. See sump pump repair for what to check.

Planning ahead for the next storm

County hazard planning records federal flood declarations for the Hanover Park area in 1972, 1973, 1986, 1987, 1993, 2013 and 2023. If your home has a basement, test the sump pump before the wet season, make sure the discharge line drains away from the foundation, and keep floor drains clear.

If water is already coming in, the first priority is safety. Stay out of a flooded basement if the water is near outlets or the electrical panel, and call us or an electrician from a dry spot.

Heavy rain also stresses older sewer lines. Slow drains in several fixtures at once can mean the building sewer is partly blocked, and a sewer inspection shows the real condition before a backup happens.

Business owners along the Irving Park Road and Lake Street corridors can use the same line. A restroom backup or a failed water heater in a shop is handled the same way, with the urgent problem stopped first and repairs planned after.

If you manage rental property in any of these towns, tell the dispatcher when you call so we can coordinate access with the tenant. Having a contact number for the occupant saves time on arrival.

Not sure whether your address is inside our coverage? Ask when you call. The dispatcher will tell you plainly.
