Cable cleaning opened the drain but it clogged again? A jetter washes the pipe wall clean, not just a hole through the middle.

Line Blocked Hard Right Now

If sewage is coming up in the lowest drain, stop all water use. Do not flush. Call us and tell us what is backing up, since a jetter is truck-mounted or trailer-sized and we need to know before we load.

Not every emergency needs a jetter. Often a cable gets the water moving, and jetting follows once the line is flowing. We decide that on site, after we hear the symptoms and, when possible, put a camera in the pipe.

What Hydro Jetting Does

A jetter pumps water through a hose with a nozzle on the end. The nozzle has rear-facing jets that pull the hose forward and forward-facing jets that cut into the clog. The spray scours the whole pipe wall, so grease, scale and silt come out instead of being pushed aside.

A cable works differently. It spins a blade through the center of the clog and leaves a hole. In a pipe coated with grease, the hole can close up within weeks. Jetting leaves the pipe with nearly its full inside diameter.

Where Jetting Fits in Older Hanover Park Pipes

Many homes here date to the 1960s and early 1970s. Their building sewers are often cast iron or clay, and the interior of cast iron is rough with rust tubercles. Jetting removes much of that scale, which is why lines cleaned this way tend to stay open longer.

Jetting is not right for every pipe. A cracked, collapsed or badly offset clay line can break further under water pressure, and a thin-walled pipe can be damaged by the wrong nozzle. That is why we recommend a sewer inspection first when the pipe is old or the history is unknown.

Roots are another reason to jet. A rotating nozzle with a cutting head trims roots at the joint and flushes the pieces out. Roots grow back, so a line with a root history should be rechecked on a schedule.

How We Run a Jetting Job

Open a cleanout

We need a clean entry point to the building sewer. If no cleanout exists, we pull a toilet or access through a roof vent. Village rules call for a cleanout to grade about five feet from the building on repairs, which also helps future jetting.

Choose the nozzle

A penetrating nozzle breaks through a dense plug. A spinning nozzle scours the wall. A root cutter trims fibrous growth. We start with the gentlest option that will work.

Work the line in passes

We advance, pull back, and repeat so debris moves toward the main instead of packing in front of the nozzle.

Verify with a camera

After the water runs clear, we run a camera when needed to confirm the pipe is open and show what the wall looks like.

Explain the findings

If we see cracks, offsets or a sag, we explain what the pipe needs next, including sewer line repair when jetting alone will not hold.

When Jetting Is the Wrong Tool

Jetting will not fix a pipe that is broken. It also cannot cure a belly, which is a low spot where water sits. If the camera shows either, clearing the line buys time but does not solve the problem.

Never jet a line you cannot see

Old clay and Orangeburg fiber pipe can fail under pressure. We prefer a camera look before jetting when the pipe age or material is unknown.

For smaller clogs in a sink or tub, cabling is faster and kinder to the fixture. Our drain cleaning service covers that work.

What Affects the Cost of Hydro Jetting

Jetting costs depend on the line and what is in it. We quote after looking at the access and, where possible, the pipe. These are the main factors.

Length of the line

A short run to the property line takes less time than a long run to the main.

Amount and type of buildup

Soft grease flushes quickly. Hard scale and thick root mats need more passes and different nozzles.

Access and cleanout location

A good cleanout makes setup quick. Without one, we may need to pull a fixture.

Pipe size and material

A four-inch line and a six-inch line need different nozzles and flow. Fragile material limits pressure.

Camera inspection

Looking before and after adds time but shows whether the work succeeded.

Local note

Hanover Park Sewer Rules That Affect Jetting

Village rules for sanitary sewer repair say pipe within about six feet of the building is six-inch, the sewer must be at least 48 inches deep, and work within five feet of the building needs a licensed plumber. A cleanout should reach grade about five feet out.

For you, this means a proper cleanout is part of a good installation. It gives us a straight path for the jetter, and it can save a repeat dig.

Relining is allowed only after the pipe has been descaled and recorded on camera, and not on defective pipe. Jetting often provides that descaling step.

Source type: Village of Hanover Park sanitary sewer repair requirements

Codes and standards that apply

Jetting sits inside the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) rules for drainage systems. If a camera shows broken pipe, the village sewer repair rules apply and a repair is not a jetting job.
