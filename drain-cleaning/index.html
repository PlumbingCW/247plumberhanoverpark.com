Drain Backing Up Right Now? Start Here

Stop running water first. Every gallon you send down a blocked line has to go somewhere, and that somewhere is often your basement floor. Skip the washing machine, dishwasher and showers until the line is open.

If several fixtures are slow at once, or the basement floor drain is bubbling, the blockage is probably in the main line and not in one trap. Do not pour drain opener into standing water. It sits on the clog, and the next person to open the pipe can get a chemical burn.

  1. Turn off appliances that drain to the same line, including the washer and dishwasher.
  2. Keep toilets unflushed if water is rising in the lowest drain in the house.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from basement floor drains.
  4. Call us, then tell us which fixtures are affected so we bring the right cable and camera.

We answer around the clock, and a main line backup is treated as an emergency whether it is Tuesday noon or Sunday at 3 a.m. For a stubborn grease or root blockage, ask about hydro jetting when we arrive.

Why Drains Clog in Hanover Park Homes

A lot of Hanover Park housing went up in the 1960s and early 1970s, when the Greenbrook subdivision was built and Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem followed. Houses from that period often have cast iron stacks, galvanized branch lines, and cast iron or clay pipe running out to the street.

Cast iron rusts from the inside and grows rough scale. Hair, grease and paper catch on that scale much faster than they do on smooth plastic. For you, that means a line that clogs again and again in the same spot is usually telling you about pipe condition, not bad habits.

Clay sewer pipe comes in short sections joined at the hubs. Over decades, roots find those joints and push in. A blockage that starts as a slow tub can turn into a full main line stoppage when a root mass catches paper. A camera tells us which one you have.

The water itself plays a part. Village supply is Lake Michigan water treated by Chicago, and reports for the same supply list hardness around 8 grains per gallon. Moderately hard water leaves scale on pipe walls and on pop-up stoppers, so narrow galvanized lines tighten over time.

How a Drain Cleaning Visit Works

Find the real problem

We ask which fixtures drain slowly and which are fine. A clog that affects only the kitchen sink is a branch problem. A clog that shows in the lowest drain is a main line problem.

Open access

We pull a cleanout cap if one is available, or remove a trap or toilet when we must. Clean access matters because a cable pushed through a tight trap can scratch it or miss the clog.

Clear the blockage

We choose a cable size and cutting head to match the pipe. A thin cable for a three-quarter inch trap arm is the wrong tool for a four-inch main.

Check the result

We run water and watch the drain, then confirm the line carries flow all the way to the main.

Look deeper if needed

If the clog returns or the cable brings back root or scale, we recommend a sewer camera inspection before it becomes a collapse.

Fixture by Fixture

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains carry grease that cools and coats the pipe wall. Each wash adds another layer until only a pinhole of flow remains. A cable breaks through the center, yet leaves grease on the walls, so heavy buildup often calls for jetting instead.

Bathroom sinks, tubs and showers

Hair is the usual culprit. It wraps around the pop-up lever or the crossbar, then traps soap and skin oils. If you pull the stopper and find a gray rope of hair, you have found the cause, but the same slime may also coat the trap beyond reach.

Toilets

A toilet that clogs with normal use may have a partial blockage in the closet bend or the branch line, not in the bowl. When a plunger fails twice, stop and call. Forcing it can push waste back up through the tub drain.

Floor drains and laundry lines

Basement floor drains sit at the lowest point, so they warn you first when the main is blocked. If one gurgles when the washer drains, the main line is already restricted. Our sewer line cleaning service handles the larger pipe beyond the house trap.

Signs the Clog Is Bigger Than One Drain

  • Two or more fixtures back up at the same time.
  • A toilet bubbles when you run the shower or washer.
  • Water appears around the basement floor drain.
  • A sewer smell shows up with no visible leak.
  • The same drain clogs again within a few weeks of being cleared.

Any of these points to the main line or the stack. Roots, a bellied section that holds water, or collapsed pipe can all cause them, and none of them respond to a bottle of drain cleaner. If the camera shows a broken pipe, we explain your options under sewer line repair.

Why we avoid chemical drain openers

Caustic openers heat up and can soften old PVC joints. They also leave a hazard behind for the next plumber, who may be working with the cap off. A cable or jetter removes the clog without leaving chemicals in your pipes.

What Affects the Cost of Drain Cleaning

The price depends on the line and the blockage, not on a flat rate. After we see the drain, we explain the work and the price before starting. These are the factors that move it.

Which drain it is

A bathroom sink trap is a short job. A main line cleaned from a distant cleanout is a longer one, with heavier equipment.

Access to the pipe

A cleanout in the yard makes the work faster. Pulling a toilet or removing a trap adds labor and may take extra time to reset.

Type of blockage

Hair and paper clear fast. Grease, roots and mineral scale need cutting heads or a jetter and more passes.

Pipe material and condition

Rough cast iron and cracked clay can snag a cutting head and slow the cleaning. Sometimes a camera pass is added to protect the pipe.

Repeat clogs

If the line has been cleared before, we look for the underlying cause with a camera. That adds a step but prevents another backup.

Keeping Drains Open After We Leave

Small habits protect older pipes. Scrape plates into the trash, not the disposal, and keep cooking oil in a can. Use a hair catcher in every shower and tub, and empty it weekly.

  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down in a clay or cast iron line.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing greasy dishes.
  • Have the main line cleaned or inspected if roots have been found before.
  • Keep the cleanout cap accessible, not buried under storage or landscaping.

If you have a basement sump or a floor drain that backs up in heavy rain, the problem may be groundwater and not a clog. That is a different repair, covered under sump pump repair.

Local note

Local Rules That Touch Drain Work

Clearing a clogged drain with a cable is routine maintenance. Replacing pipe is different. The village requires plumbing contractors to be registered, and permits are not issued until a contractor is registered with Inspectional Services.

Illinois licenses plumbers through the state health department under the Illinois Plumbing Code, and IDPH offers an online license lookup. For you, that means you can verify any plumber before they open a wall or dig up your lateral.

Inspectional Services is open Monday through Friday from 8 to 4:30 and is closed on Wednesdays. If your job needs a permit, plan the call around that schedule.

Source type: Village of Hanover Park Inspectional Services and the Illinois Department of Public Health

Safety before you start

Never mix drain cleaners, and never pour one into standing water that may contain another chemical. Open a window, wear gloves and eye protection, and keep children and pets away. If a product splashes on skin or eyes, rinse with water and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222. If sewage is backing up, stop using water and call us.

Codes and standards that apply

Drain work in Illinois falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890), and only licensed plumbers may repair or alter drain piping. The Illinois Department of Public Health keeps an online license lookup, so you can check any plumber before work starts.
