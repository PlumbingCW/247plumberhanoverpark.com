Roselle history and what it means for your plumbing

Roselle was incorporated in 1922 as a bedroom community, and its Metra station on the Milwaukee District West line pulled commuters to Chicago. The population grew from 3,581 in 1960 to 17,034 in 1980, according to the standard reference page. Most homes date from those decades.

In practical terms, that makes houses here close in age to those in Hanover Park. Expect older shutoff valves, toilets with worn fill valves, and water heaters near the end of their lives. Replacing a worn valve early costs far less than cleaning up after a failure.

Toilets, fixtures, and small leaks that grow

A running toilet wastes water all day and can raise the bill. Often the flapper or fill valve is worn. A rocking toilet may have a failed wax ring, which leaks at the floor. Our toilet repair visits fix these on the first trip in most cases.

Dripping faucets, loose handles, and slow shower flow are also common. Scale from hard water is a frequent cause, and cleaning or replacing the cartridge restores pressure.

Quick steps for a Roselle burst pipe or toilet overflow.

Close the main valve

Drain the lines

Soak up water quickly and run a fan to slow mold growth.

Mark the spot

Note where the leak started so the plumber can go straight to it.

Local note

Spring Creek and the Roselle landscape

Spring Creek is a tributary of the East Branch of the DuPage River and flows through Roselle, according to the standard reference page. Goose Lake and Turner Pond, a man-made pond just north of the town center, also hold water in the village.

For homeowners, that means the water table can rise after long rain. If your basement is near the creek or a pond, check that your sump pump runs and the discharge line is clear. Roselle is a DuPage Water Commission partner, so water comes from Lake Michigan like nearby towns.

Water heaters in Roselle homes

A water heater that pools at the base, makes popping sounds, or runs out of hot water quickly is warning you. Popping usually means scale on the tank bottom. A puddle means the tank has rusted through, and only replacement fixes that.

If you smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call the gas utility first. For any other heater trouble, we respond at any hour and can often tell you on the phone whether a repair is realistic. Have the tank's age and the brand label ready, since both help us bring the right parts.
