Before We Arrive

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way. None of them need tools.

Find the main shutoff

Locate the main water valve, usually near the meter in the basement or utility room. Turn it clockwise until it stops if water is spraying or pooling.

Stop the heater

For a leaking water heater, turn the gas control to pilot or off, or flip the breaker for an electric unit. Then close the cold water valve on top.

Move belongings

Lift boxes, rugs, and electronics off the floor. Standing water ruins cardboard and drywall bottoms fast.

Stay out of standing water

If water is near outlets or the panel, do not step in. Cut power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Photograph the damage

Take clear photos of leaks and wet areas before you clean up. They help later if you file an insurance claim.

Local note

A depot that gave Bartlett its name

Bartlett is named for Luther Bartlett, who in 1873 gave money and half of his 40-acre woodlot toward a train depot. The 1873 station still stands and now holds the Bartlett Depot Museum, according to the village and the standard reference history. The village was incorporated in 1892.

That history shows why the oldest blocks near the depot have older service lines than the subdivisions built later. If your house is close to the center of town, ask us to check the line where it enters the building. Newer subdivisions further out usually need attention to water heaters and drains first.
