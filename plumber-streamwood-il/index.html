Why Streamwood plumbing ages differently

Streamwood was incorporated on February 9, 1957. Its population jumped from 4,821 in 1960 to 18,176 in 1970, the census shows. In practical terms, a big share of homes here were built within the same decade, so they reach the same plumbing milestones together.

Houses from that boom often have cast iron or clay sewer laterals, galvanized supply lines in older additions, and a basement floor drain that has not been cleaned in years. Those parts wear out on their own schedule. When one fails, it often hints that neighboring parts are close behind.

Sewer and drain trouble in 1960s homes

Tree roots find their way into clay joints and cast iron seams. The first sign is usually a slow drain, then a gurgle from the basement floor drain. If water backs up into the lowest fixture, the main is blocked. A camera pass with sewer inspection shows whether roots, a belly, or a broken joint is the cause.

Once we know the cause, the choice is cleaning, spot repair, or a full line fix. Roots are cleared with sewer line cleaning. A crushed or collapsed section needs sewer line repair.

Before We Arrive

A few quick actions keep a Streamwood sewer or leak problem from spreading.

Stop using water

Do not run the dishwasher, washer, or showers if a drain is backing up. Every gallon you send down adds to the overflow.

Clear a path

Move items away from the basement floor drain, cleanout, and water heater so the plumber can reach them.

Close the main valve

If a pipe is leaking, shut off the main water valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines.

Protect valuables

Lift stored boxes, rugs, and electronics off the floor in the basement.

Note what happened

Write down which fixtures gurgled or backed up and when. This helps the plumber find the blockage faster.

Water heaters that have outlived their first owners

Few original water heaters survive from the 1960s, but many homes are on their second or third. A tank that leaks at the seam cannot be fixed and needs replacement. If the burner or thermostat is the issue, a repair may be enough. We check the tank age and condition before suggesting either path.

Streamwood water and the cross-connection program

Streamwood receives Chicago Lake Michigan water through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency, the same supply that reaches Hanover Park. The village Public Works department runs a cross-connection program, which protects the public water system from backflow.

In practical terms, if you have a lawn irrigation system, a boiler fill line, or a hose connection that touches chemicals, the village may require a backflow device. Ask us when we are on site and we can point out what to expect. Public Works can confirm village requirements.

Floor drains, laundry stacks, and slow lines

A floor drain that has sat dry for months can let sewer gas into the basement. Pour a bucket of water down it to refill the trap. If water drains slowly or gurgles, the branch line behind it is partly blocked and needs cleaning before it closes completely.

Laundry standpipes are another weak point in houses of that era. Lint and soap build a sticky coat inside the pipe, and a washer pumping out fast can overflow the standpipe onto the floor. A routine cleaning every few years keeps it clear.
