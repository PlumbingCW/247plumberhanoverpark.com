Disposal stopped or leaking: do this first

Turn the wall switch off. Then switch off the breaker for the disposal if you can find it. Never put your hand in the chamber, even when the unit is off.

If water is leaking under the sink, put a bowl underneath and note whether it comes from the top flange, the side drain or the bottom of the unit. A leak from the bottom or a seam in the housing usually means the unit has failed and needs replacing.

Why disposals fail

  • A jam from a bone, fruit pit, bottle cap or a spoon that slipped down the drain.
  • A hum with no spin. The motor is getting power but the flywheel is stuck.
  • Silence and no hum, which often means a tripped reset button or a dead outlet.
  • A leak at the sink flange where the plumber's putty or gasket has dried out.
  • A leak at the dishwasher hose connection, where the clamp has loosened.
  • Slow drainage because grease and food paste have coated the drain line past the unit.

Most jams clear with a hex wrench turned into the socket on the underside of the unit. If you free it and it jams again, something is wrong with the grinding plate or the line.

How we repair a disposal

Kill power and test the unit

We confirm there is power, press the reset button on the base and listen to how the motor behaves.

Free the flywheel

We turn the shaft by hand with the wrench from below, then remove any object from the chamber with tongs.

Check the seals and connections

We inspect the sink flange, the mounting ring, the discharge elbow and the dishwasher inlet for weeping joints.

Clear the line past the disposal

If the sink still drains slowly, the problem is in the trap arm or the branch line. We clear it or recommend drain cleaning.

What Hanover Park kitchens deal with

The homes in Hanover Park's Greenbrook, Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem subdivisions date from the 1960s and early 1970s. Kitchen drain lines in that period were often galvanized steel or cast iron, and the interiors scale over time. A disposal adds food particles to a line that is already narrowing. For lines that keep returning, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall. This is why one kitchen sink can clog repeatedly while the disposal works fine.

If the sink backs up and other fixtures gurgle or back up too, the problem may be in the main line, not the disposal. Sewer trouble in older homes may call for a camera sewer inspection before you spend more on kitchen repairs.

What affects garbage disposal repair cost

A disposal job can end with a quick reset or a full replacement. These points decide where it lands.

Repair or replace

Freeing a jam is simple. A cracked housing or a burned motor means a new unit.

Horsepower and model

A larger, quieter unit with sound insulation costs more than a basic one.

Mounting and plumbing fit

Different mounting systems may need a new ring, flange or discharge elbow.

Electrical connection

A hardwired unit or one without a nearby outlet takes more work than a plug-in.

Drain line condition

Grease buildup in the trap arm or branch line adds a cleaning step.

Insurance policies treat sudden water damage differently from slow leaks. A disposal that has leaked for weeks under a cabinet may not be covered, so check your policy.

Call and tell us what the unit does. We will explain whether repair or replacement makes sense before we start.

Habits that keep a disposal running

Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after. Skip grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice, celery and potato peels. Those items pack into a paste that sticks inside the drain.

Never use chemical drain openers with a disposal. They sit in the chamber and attack seals and metal. If the sink drains slowly, call us instead. Replacing a unit is usually a repair job, but adding a new circuit or moving drain lines can need a permit from the village Inspectional Services Division.

Codes and standards that apply

Disposal drain connections follow the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). The electrical side of a disposal is separate from plumbing and should be disconnected at the switch or breaker before any repair.
