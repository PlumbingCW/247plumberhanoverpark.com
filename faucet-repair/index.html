Faucet spraying or flooding: first steps

Look under the sink and turn the two small oval handles clockwise. Hot is on the left and cold is on the right. If a handle will not turn, close the main shut-off where the water line enters the house.

Empty the cabinet so the wood base does not soak. Put a bowl under any drip and wipe the area dry. Water running along the pipes toward the floor is a sign the supply connector has split, and it should be replaced, not taped.

Know your faucet before you fix it

The repair depends on the internal design, so identifying the type saves time and parts.

Compression faucets

These have separate hot and cold handles that turn several times. A rubber washer presses on a brass seat. When the washer hardens or the seat pits, the faucet drips.

Cartridge and ball faucets

Single-handle faucets use a cartridge or a slotted ball with rubber seats and springs. O-rings on the spout swivel can also leak. Those parts wear out with use, and scale speeds it up.

Ceramic disc faucets

Two polished discs slide against each other inside a cylinder. They last a long time but crack or clog with grit, and then the handle feels gritty or leaks from the base.

What we do on a faucet repair visit

Shut off and open the faucet

We close the stops, release pressure and take the handle apart. We note the brand and model so the correct cartridge goes in.

Inspect the seats and the body

A new washer on a pitted seat will drip again within days. We resurface or replace the seat, or replace the whole faucet when the body is corroded.

Check supply lines and stops

Rubber and braided lines age out. We replace weak connectors and any angle stop that drips when turned.

Flush debris and test

We run the faucet with the aerator off to clear grit, refit the aerator and check for drips under load.

Hanover Park homes and faucet wear

Hanover Park grew fast in the 1960s and early 1970s, with the Greenbrook subdivision first and Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem soon after. Many kitchens and baths have had a faucet swapped, but the stop valves and copper or galvanized supply stubs behind the wall are often original. A new faucet on a seized stop is a common reason we replace the valve too.

Water here is Lake Michigan water supplied through the Joint Action Water Agency. Reports for the same Chicago supply, such as Streamwood's 2023 report, show about 8 grains per gallon of hardness. That level builds white scale inside aerators and cartridges, so a faucet that slowly loses pressure may need cleaning before anyone replaces it.

If low pressure shows up at every tap, the cause may be elsewhere, and leak detection can check the lines. Supply trouble in the yard is a job for water line repair.

What affects faucet repair cost

A faucet visit can be small or involve opening a wall. These factors decide how big the job is.

Faucet type and brand

Common cartridges are easy to source. Specialty or imported fixtures can need ordered parts.

Condition of the seats and body

A corroded body or deep scale may make replacement a better value than repair.

Shut-off valve condition

If the stops will not close, they have to be replaced to isolate the fixture.

Location

A kitchen faucet with a sprayer and a filter line takes longer than a basic bathroom faucet.

Water pressure and hardness

High pressure or heavy scale shortens part life and may call for a pressure check.

Homeowners policies usually respond to sudden water damage, but slow drips that stain cabinets over months are often treated as maintenance. Read your own policy.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

A dripping outdoor spigot is a faucet too. A cracked bib after a hard freeze leaks inside the wall once the hose is attached. Shut off the interior stop and call if you see water on the basement ceiling. A split pipe behind the wall may need burst pipe repair.

Replacing a faucet does not need a permit. Moving supply lines or rerouting piping may. Hanover Park's Inspectional Services Division issues plumbing permits, and registered plumbing contractors handle them.

Codes and standards that apply

Faucet and supply-line repairs fall under the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890). The Illinois Department of Public Health keeps an online license lookup, so you can check any plumber before work starts.
