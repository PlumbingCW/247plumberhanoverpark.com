A small camera on a flexible cable shows what is inside your sewer pipe. You see the problem, not just hear about it.

What a Sewer Camera Shows

A sewer inspection sends a waterproof camera head down your building sewer from a cleanout. The camera records what it sees and the screen shows depth and distance. We watch for roots, cracks, offset joints, scale, sags and collapsed sections.

This is the only way to see pipe condition without digging. A cable tells us something is in the way. A camera tells us what it is, where it is, and whether the pipe around it is sound.

When to Schedule One

  • Your drains clog more than once a year.
  • A sewer backup happened and you want to know why.
  • You are buying or selling a house built before the 1980s.
  • You plan to finish a basement or landscape over the sewer path.
  • A previous plumber said roots were present.
  • You are considering lining and need the village-required camera record.

If you are in the middle of a backup, call us for sewer line cleaning first. We clear the line and then inspect it, since the camera cannot see through standing water.

Why Camera Work Matters in Older Housing

The 1960s boom built Greenbrook, and Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem followed in the early 1970s. Those homes often have clay or cast iron sewers now 50 years old. A camera is the quickest way to learn whether yours is fine or near the end.

For a buyer, that is useful. A pipe replacement is a large expense, and finding out before closing gives you room to negotiate or plan. For an owner, it helps decide between cleaning on a schedule and fixing the pipe.

How the Inspection Goes

Find access

We use a cleanout when you have one. If not, we pull a toilet or use a roof vent. A cleanout is worth adding if you do not have one.

Run the camera

The head travels toward the street. We watch the screen and note distances for any defect.

Locate problems

A locator above ground marks where damage sits and how deep, so a repair can be planned.

Review with you

We show the footage and explain what each defect means in plain words.

Provide the record

You receive the recording and our notes. Keep them for your files and for any contractor who bids a repair.

The Village Camera Requirement

Hanover Park's sanitary sewer repair rules say relining needs descaling and a recorded camera inspection, and relining is not allowed on defective pipe. A recording is therefore part of any lining job. Our footage helps document what the pipe looked like before work began.

If the camera shows damage, see sewer line repair for what replacement involves. If it shows only buildup, hydro jetting may restore flow.

What Affects the Cost of a Sewer Inspection

Camera inspections are priced by the work involved. We confirm the scope when you call. These factors matter.

Access point

A cleanout makes it quick. Pulling a toilet to reach the line adds time.

Length of the line

A longer run to the main takes more cable and more time to review.

Pipe condition

Heavy scale or debris may need cleaning before the camera can pass.

Locating and mapping

Marking depth and position above ground is extra work but helps plan any repair.

Report and recording

A saved video and notes take time to prepare and are useful for buyers, insurers and contractors.

Questions Buyers and Owners Ask the Village

Inspectional Services can tell you about permits on file for an address. It is open Monday through Friday from 8 to 4:30 and closed on Wednesdays. Past permits show whether a sewer was replaced.

The village also keeps a service line inventory dashboard on its water quality page. That covers water service lines, not sewers, so use it for the supply side and the camera for the drain side.
