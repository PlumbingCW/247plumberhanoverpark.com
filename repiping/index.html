Repiping in Hanover Park, IL

Rusty water, weak pressure and repeat leaks usually mean the pipes are at the end of their life. Repiping replaces them so you stop paying for patches.

Call (630) 822-9290

When repiping makes sense

Repiping means replacing the supply lines that carry water to your faucets, appliances and water heater. It is not a drain job. Plumbers usually recommend it when repairs start to repeat, or when the pipe itself is the problem and not one bad joint.

  • Brown or yellow water, especially after the tap sat unused
  • Pressure that has faded over the years
  • A second or third pinhole leak in the same year
  • Visible green corrosion on copper or flaking rust on steel
  • Galvanized pipe in a home built in the 1960s or 1970s
  • Water that tastes metallic

One pinhole in a copper line is a repair. Several pinholes in different rooms suggest the whole run has thinned. Our leak detection service can find how many sections are failing before you decide.

How a repipe goes

Walk the house

We check the water line entry, meter, water heater and every fixture, and mark where new lines will run.

Plan access

We choose where to open drywall, typically in closets, behind fixtures and in basement or crawl space ceilings.

Shut down and replace

We close the water, remove old supply pipe and run new lines to every fixture. Most homes are without water for part of the day, not for days.

Test under pressure

New lines are pressurized and inspected for leaks before any wall is closed.

Inspection and patching

The village inspects the work. After that, we close the openings, and drywall finishing is up to you or a handyman.

Homes with a basement and unfinished ceilings often go faster, since the lines are visible. Finished slabs or tight two-story layouts take more planning. We tell you the access plan before we start.

Repiping in older Hanover Park homes

Much of Hanover Park was built in the 1960s boom. Greenbrook went up first, then Woodlake Trails and Olde Salem in the early 1970s. Homes from those years commonly have galvanized steel or early copper plumbing. Galvanized pipe rusts inside, narrows over time and can restrict flow to a fraction of its original size.

The village uses 100% Lake Michigan water supplied by the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency. Reports for the same Chicago supply describe moderately hard water, around 8 grains per gallon. Hard water leaves mineral deposits in older pipe and in fixtures, so repiping is a good time to consider a water softener too.

Lead and the service line

Repiping usually stops at the meter, but the line from the street matters. Illinois requires suppliers to inventory lead service lines, and the village keeps a service line inventory dashboard on its water quality page. If your line is lead, the law says a repair means replacing the entire line, so plan that along with the interior work.

A new service line is a separate project, covered in water line repair. Doing both together can reduce disruption and tear-up.

What affects the cost of repiping

No two houses are the same. These factors drive the scope.

Size of the house

More bathrooms, kitchens and fixtures mean more pipe and more connections.

Material choice

Copper and PEX differ in material, labor and fittings.

Access

Open basement ceilings are straightforward. Finished walls, slabs and tight chases take longer to reach.

Drywall and finish work

Cutting access holes is part of the job, and patching and painting may be separate.

Permits and inspection

Whole-house work needs a village permit and an inspection from Inspectional Services.

Repiping to replace worn pipe is generally a maintenance cost and not an insured loss, though damage from a sudden burst may be covered. Ask your carrier before you decide.

Local note

Permits and licensed work for repiping

Plumbing work in the village goes through the Inspectional Services Division at 2121 Lake Street. Contractors need village registration, which requires IDPH 055 contractor registration and a 058 plumber licence, and the permit is not issued until that is in place. The Illinois Plumbing Code is 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890.

Owner-occupants may use a Homeowner Plumbing Affidavit to do their own work, and then may only hire Illinois-licensed plumbers to assist. For a whole-house repipe, most owners bring in a licensed contractor because the job is long and the inspections are strict.

Source type: Hanover Park Inspectional Services and Illinois IDPH

Codes and standards that apply
